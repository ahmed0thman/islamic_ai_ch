SOURCE TALK (decision 083, owner's rule): the woven text carries the meaning; who said it, in which book, with what grading and in what state belongs to the marker and its record. The rule is brevity, not removal:
- A scholar's name in its shortest form («عند ابن عاشور», «قال السعدي», «يرى الطبري»), a disagreement told by its holders' names, and a narrator or citer named in a few words before a narration are NOT findings.
- A finding (kind `other`, with the `problem` text starting "source-talk:"): a standalone sentence or clause whose subject is the source or the state (who narrated it, where it is found, whose wording it is, that it is the scholar's own statement, why it is weak, that we did not judge it); a long status phrase inside a sentence («لم نحكم على ثبوته», «وليس معنا فيه رواية محكوم عليها», «خبر من قوله»); or more than a few words of names, book titles and transmission vocabulary in one sentence. The exporter's warning W17 lists candidates (`python3 -B tools/export_content.py {{surah_no}} --check-only`); judge each, and look for what it cannot catch (a source sentence that names no one).

PLAIN LANGUAGE (decision 087, owner's rule): the reader has no training in Arabic linguistics or in the Islamic sciences. Levels 0, 1 and 2 (stop titles, questions, summaries, and the short in-place explanation of a term) are written in familiar words, at about the level of a middle-school pupil; level 3 is where the difficult words belong. - A finding (kind `other`, with the `problem` text starting "plain-language:", severity `minor`; `major` when the reader cannot follow the sentence without the word): in system text of levels 0-2, a literary, rare or technical word where a familiar one carries the same meaning (examples from surah 93: «يُنبئ عن», «يتضمن», «أجلّ», «متهكمةً», «مشوبة بالمضارّ», «استغناءً», «النحاة»); a word explained by a harder word; a term whose name comes with no plain meaning before it, or whose in-place explanation runs longer than a few words; an ayah word left with no plain gloss anywhere in level 1; colloquial dialect instead of plain standard Arabic.

A sentence cut or shortened in the name of simplification, so that information is lost, is `unsupported` too.
